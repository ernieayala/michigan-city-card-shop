# Distribution and Publisher Access for Sealed TCG Product

> Last updated: 2026-10-01

## Headline

The owner's belief holds in part, and the split matters:

1. **Opening a distributor account is not the hard part for a physical store.** Southern Hobby, GTS and PHD publish a survey, a document list, and a 3 to 10 business day review. A storefront with signage, a resale certificate and a play area clears it.
2. **Opening an account is close to impossible for an online-only seller.** Southern Hobby, PHD and (per a search snippet) Peachstate refuse online-only applicants in writing. GTS limits "brick and mortar exclusive" product to on-street stores. Every publisher store program found (Play! Pokémon, WPN, Lorcana HSP, Konami OTS, Bandai TCG+ store, Star Wars Unlimited) requires a physical storefront, and most also require seating. Star Wars: Unlimited states only a brick-and-mortar requirement (section 1.7).
3. **The hard part is allocation quantity on hot product, and a new store starts at the back of the line.** Distributors allocate Pokémon and new One Piece sets from purchase history and organized-play activity. Reported outcomes for hobby stores run from less than 10% to 15% of the order (Prismatic Evolutions, Jan 2025) down to one Elite Trainer Box (30th Celebration, Sep 2026). The Pokémon Company now owns the largest US Pokémon distributor, which serves mass retail.
4. **Buying on the secondary market to fill the gap loses money or earns thin margins.** Worked example below: an OP-16 One Piece booster box bought by the case at TCGplayer market price and resold on TCGplayer nets **-$10.01 per box** before postage.

No source found gives a TCG-specific distributor discount off MSRP. That number drives every sealed-product margin in any financial model and is the first thing to get from a distributor call.

## 1. Publisher retailer programs

### 1.1 Pokémon: Play! Pokémon Store

**Finding:** A Play! Pokémon Store must be a commercial venue with a cashier desk, product shelving, a store window, exterior signage, public daytime hours, seating for at least 8, and must sell sealed Pokémon TCG product from current or recent sets.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://www.pokemon.com/us/play-pokemon/organize/event-requirements> ; handbook: <https://www.pokemon.com/static-assets/content-assets/cms2/pdf/play-pokemon/rules/play-pokemon-store-handbook-en.pdf> (last revised 2026-05-21)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

The handbook adds: no cost to join; a Pokémon Trainer Central account; a venue review with photo and video (AI-generated media "may disqualify your application"); an account review that may include a background check. Prerelease eligibility needs reporting in two of the three prior League seasons. Prerelease kits and promo materials scale with reported attendance: "The number of participants in each report directly influences the amount of promotional material." False reporting can bring "decreased allocations."

The program governs events and promo material. It does not sell sealed product; sealed product comes from distributors. The handbook lists US Prerelease resellers: ACD, Alliance, All Sports Mktg., Entertainment Earth, Gold River Distribution, GTS Distribution, HAMPS Distribution, Mad Al Distribution, Magazine Exchange Inc., Peachstate Hobby Distribution, Southern Hobby, Sweet Deal Distribution (Appendix A, "subject to change").

An online-only seller cannot qualify: the venue criteria require a window, signage and public hours.

**Disconfirming check:** Looked for any statement that an online seller or home venue qualifies. Found none. The TPCi onboarding sheet hosted by GTS (dated March 2024, <https://gtsdistribution.com/images/pku_leagueonboardingandmaintenance_final.pdf>) says the Organized Play team checks the store's Google listing photos against venue requirements, which points the other way.

### 1.2 Magic: The Gathering: Wizards Play Network (WPN)

**Finding:** WPN membership requires an open storefront that does not share space with another business, a dedicated play space seating 8 or more, and a video walkthrough showing exterior signage, the point of sale, at least four displays of Magic product from the last two years, the play space and restrooms.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://wpn.wizards.com/en/news/how-apply-wpn>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Ownership proof: business license, tax ID, articles of organization, executed lease, or similar, showing the physical address. WotC states processing takes 1 to 3 business days for complete applications. The page does not say whether WPN membership is a condition for buying Magic from a distributor. Prerelease product and promo allocations run through WPN; WotC finalizes promo allocations 2 to 3 months before scheduled delivery, so a new member's first allocations trail its approval by at least that long.

**Disconfirming check:** Searched for an online-only path into WPN. The WPN page is silent on online-only; the storefront, play space and signage video requirements exclude it in practice. GTS terms (section 2.2) name Wizards of the Coast as a vendor that forbids sub-distribution, so buying Magic from another store to resell also breaks distributor terms.

### 1.3 Magic: WPN Premium and the 2026 tier change

**Finding:** WPN Premium requires 1,500 tickets, 30 engaged players and 10 activated players a year (about 29 tickets a week), a floor plan, a secret-shopper score of 90% or higher, an online storefront, and a walkthrough video. WotC announced on 2026-03-04 a six-tier Premium structure launching at the end of 2026.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://wpn.wizards.com/en/premium-hub> ; <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/61787/wizards-coast-announces-changes-wpn-program>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Premium stores get exclusive products and larger event allocations. The ICv2 report gives no tier requirements for the new structure. A new store would not reach Premium in year one unless it sells about 29 event tickets a week from opening; that is a demand question for [storefront costs](storefront-costs-and-demand.md).

**Disconfirming check:** Older WPN figures (3,000 / 60 / 20 in 2019, cut to 2,000 / 40 / 15 in Nov 2019) show the bar has fallen twice. It could fall again under the 2026 tiers. No tier thresholds found yet.

### 1.4 One Piece Card Game (Bandai): TCG+ store

**Finding:** Bandai sells One Piece only through official distributors (US: GTS Distribution, Universal Distribution USA, PHD Games, Southern Hobby Distribution). A store with organized-play space for 8 or more "can apply as a Brick and Mortar store" via Bandai TCG+ corporate membership, and receives a Welcome Kit through its distributor.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/forstore/>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

The page does not describe any path for online-only sellers. TCG+ store status is what Bandai uses for reprint priority (section 3.4).

**Disconfirming check:** Looked for a Bandai statement that registration is not required to buy product. PHD's 2026-08-03 Bandai registration guide (<https://www.phdgames.com/2026/08/03/bandai-tabletop-games-official-store-registration-guide/>) covers Gundam event registration and does not address purchase eligibility. Unresolved; see Still unknown.

### 1.5 Disney Lorcana (Ravensburger): Hobby Store Program (HSP)

**Finding:** Lorcana HSP members must occupy a finished (not under construction) commercial property not nested in another business, recognizable as a retail location focused on hobby gaming, open to the public including minors, stocked with sealed product from at least two game categories, with a dedicated business phone. Organized-play locations need permanent seating for 12 and a bathroom.
**Confidence:** sourced (document dated 2023-05-10, older than 18 months)
**Source:** <https://cdn.ravensburger.com/lorcana/store-requirements-en> ; agreement: <https://cdn.ravensburger.com/lorcana/hsp-agreement-en>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

The HSP agreement adds: membership "will be cancelled if you stop purchasing Disney Lorcana TCG products from an authorized distributor for a period of ninety 90 consecutive days"; no sale before the first sale date; Ravensburger may terminate "with or without cause." ICv2 (2023, <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/54074/icv2-exclusive-ravensburgers-hobby-store-program-applications-go-live-melee>) reported acceptance criteria included time in business and that the number of stores accepted "will be limited."

**Disconfirming check:** The requirements document is from May 2023, older than 18 months. No newer version found. Treat as `sourced` until re-checked.

### 1.6 Yu-Gi-Oh! (Konami): Official Tournament Store (OTS)

**Finding:** OTS requires a physical retail location with permanent exterior signage, seating for 16, at least one reported tournament a month, and "a wide selection" of Yu-Gi-Oh! product. OTS stores may sell new product 2 days before street date.
**Confidence:** fact (publisher-sponsored ICv2 post, 2025-08-12)
**Source:** <https://icv2.com/articles/sponsored/view/60272/sponsored-become-official-tournament-store-yu-gi-oh-trading-card-game>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Contact is us-opsupport@konami.com. No Konami page retrieved names its US distributors; Southern Hobby lists Konami as a vendor (search result, page not fetched).

**Disconfirming check:** ICv2 (Scott Thorne, 2023-11-13, <https://icv2.com/articles/columns/view/55567/rolling-initiative-the-recent-decline-tcg-sales-retail-level>) wrote that Yu-Gi-Oh! booster boxes "can readily be found online the date of release below cost or a few percentage points above." If still true, Yu-Gi-Oh! access is easy and margin is the problem. That figure is older than 18 months.

### 1.7 Star Wars: Unlimited, Riftbound, Dragon Ball Super Fusion World

**Finding:** Star Wars: Unlimited organized play is open to "brick and mortar" game stores that set up a retailer account through their Asmodee distributor. Riftbound product is "only available through our authorized distributors," and organized play needs a store application on the Riftbound OP site.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://starwarsunlimited.com/how-to-stock> ; <https://starwarsunlimited.com/host-events> (via search result) ; <https://assets.ctfassets.net/qgsv4hbp414k/1cyN1XKIhPnFrzByJSaqu2/4061eb54b8c0917aaeeafa92c25cb0a2/Riftbound_Retailer_FAQ.pdf> (2025-10-31)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Riftbound: every eligible store got the same launch promo kit (3 Nexus Night kits, 144 promo packs); future promo quantity will follow "correctly logged in-store event activity." Dragon Ball Super Fusion World: no Bandai page retrieved; it runs on the same Bandai TCG+ system as One Piece, so the same storefront rule likely applies. Not verified.

**Disconfirming check:** ICv2 (2025-11-07, <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/60972/riftbound-launch-driving-traffic-sales>) quotes UVS: "some hold-back volumes to support Organized Play stores and others sending out 100% of their stock ASAP." So Riftbound allocation varies by distributor, and at least some favor OP stores.

## 2. Distributors: account requirements

### 2.1 Southern Hobby Distribution

**Finding:** Southern Hobby accepts applications only from brick-and-mortar stores and states that surveys "from online-only businesses or non-traditional retail setups, such as warehouses, office suites, or businesses operating within another business, will not be considered."
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://www.southernhobby.com/new_account.php>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Requirements: permanent physical location (no kiosks or market stalls), 1 exterior photo with signage, 4 interior photos, then business license and sales tax / resale certificate. Review up to 10 business days. New customers face restricted access to certain CCGs: "allocations will be based on past sales" with "no guarantees regarding product availability." Minimum order and credit terms not stated.

**Disconfirming check:** Looked for an exception path for new stores or online sellers on the page. None.

### 2.2 GTS Distribution

**Finding:** GTS allocates "limited availability products among its customers at any time for any reason," processes initial orders on credit card, and restricts "brick and mortar exclusive" products to "a physical on-street retail store" open multiple days a week; "stores located within a building used as a residence, flea market spaces, and 'pop-up stores' do not qualify."
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://www.gtsdistribution.com/images/GTS_Terms-of-sale.pdf> (revised 2026-04-23)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Other terms: pre-screen survey, then application with resale certificates; 3 to 5 business days. Free shipping on orders of $750 or more from one warehouse; $9.50 handling per invoice; $6.00 residential fee. Listed prices are cash-discount prices, 2.5% below regular pricing. Accounts can be terminated for "failure to meet minimum spend levels" and "a period of inactivity." B&M-exclusive buyers must not list prohibited items on third-party websites. GTS terminates accounts that resell to anyone but end users and names Wizards of the Coast as a vendor forbidding it. GTS's MAP page (<https://www.gtsdistribution.com/info/map.asp>) lists 33 board-game publishers and no TCG publisher.

**Disconfirming check:** The GTS account page does not say online-only sellers are refused outright. An online seller might open a general account; GTS's own terms bar it from B&M-exclusive lines. GTS does not publish which TCG products carry the B&M-exclusive flag. Ask.

### 2.3 PHD Games

**Finding:** PHD states: "We are currently not able to accept any new gaming account applications for online-only stores, non-gaming brick and mortar, or international retailers."
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://www.phdgames.com/pre-qualification-application/> ; <https://www.phdgames.com/faqs/>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Pre-qualification needs 5 photos including "the open play area where you host events/tournaments," plus a utility bill or lease. FAQ: minimum order $400 for gaming items; new retailers pay by credit card; terms need "one year of business banking history and 6 months of buying history from PHD." Free ground shipping at $750 from one warehouse. "Wholesale discounts are usually calculated based off of the SRP… The discount varies depending on several factors." No discount figure published. A search snippet said PHD had paused all new gaming accounts until "the fall"; the page fetched today did not show that line. Not verified.

**Disconfirming check:** None of PHD's pages suggests an online-only exception.

### 2.4 Peachstate Hobby Distribution

**Finding:** A search-index snippet attributes to Peachstate the same wording as PHD refusing online-only and non-gaming applicants, with a storefront photo required.
**Confidence:** guess (page not retrieved; snippet may conflate with PHD's identical text)
**Source:** not retrieved; search result only
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Peachstate is on Pokémon's US Prerelease reseller list (section 1.1).

**Disconfirming check:** Could not fetch a Peachstate account page. Call to confirm.

### 2.5 Alliance Game Distributors (now Universal Distribution)

**Finding:** Universal Distribution completed its purchase of Alliance Game Distributors out of the Diamond bankruptcy on or before 2025-05-16; alliance-games.com now serves Universal's site.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/59575/under-new-ownership-alliance-diamond>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

The older Alliance New Account FAQ (<https://www.alliance-games.com/Downloads/Alliance_New_Account_FAQ.PDF>) could not be retrieved (TLS error, then a redirect to Universal's homepage). A search snippet of it said Alliance was "not accepting applications from retailers whose primary interest is in collectible card game (CCG) products that are regularly allocated by manufacturers," with restrictions on new accounts ordering those items. Those terms match Upper Deck's Certified Diamond Dealer program ([sports-cards.md](sports-cards.md)), and Universal is an authorized Upper Deck distributor, so the snippet likely describes that sports-only dealer program, not a Universal TCG policy. ## 3. Allocation

### 3.1 Pokémon: Prismatic Evolutions (Jan 2025)

**Finding:** A PokeBeach poll of US stores found the average store received "less than 10% to 15% of what they requested"; a store asking for 100 ETBs "might say they are only allowed to buy 15," while PokeBeach expected Target, Walmart and Best Buy to receive their typical allotment.
**Confidence:** sourced (fan news site, 2025-01-04; poll size and method not published)
**Source:** <https://www.pokebeach.com/2025/01/prismatic-evolutions-allocations-severely-cut-to-local-stores-demand-higher-than-supply>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

The quoted store owner says distributors give "loyal Pokemon stores a higher percentage" but spread product across as many accounts as possible "to retain as many accounts as possible." A new store is by definition not a loyal Pokémon account.

**Disconfirming check:** TPCi said it would print more (per press coverage; primary statement not retrieved). A reprint adds supply months later. The launch-window share a new store gets stays the same.

### 3.2 Pokémon: 30th Celebration (Sep 2026)

**Finding:** ICv2 columnist and retailer Scott Thorne reports that for the 30th Celebration set "one distributor got the store one ETB; not one case," while Sam's Club sold two ETBs per person to a line "a block long," and that most distributors now run allocation on a take-it-or-it-goes-to-a-competitor basis.
**Confidence:** sourced (trade column, one retailer's account, 2026-09-28)
**Source:** <https://icv2.com/articles/columns/view/63479/rolling-initiative-glut-pokmon-tcg-30th-celebration-not-hobby-retail>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

This is two days old and describes the current set. It says TPCi directs "most of the product to mass market stores."

**Disconfirming check:** ICv2 (2026-08-28, <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/63244/pokemon-tcg-shows-signs-cooling-off-from-hot-summer-sales>) reports Pokémon secondary prices falling from launch, which would ease pressure if it continues. It does not show hobby allocation improving.

### 3.3 Pokémon: vertical integration into distribution

**Finding:** TPCi agreed on or before 2026-02-22 to acquire Excell Brands, which ICv2 describes as "the largest U.S. distributor of Pokemon TCG," serving Target, Walmart, Meijer, Barnes & Noble and Amazon.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/61690/the-pokmon-company-international-acquiring-excell-brands>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

The publisher now owns the pipe to mass retail. TPCi said Excell would keep its "independent approach." No evidence yet on how hobby distributor allocations change.

**Disconfirming check:** Looked for a post-acquisition change to hobby distribution. None found.

### 3.4 One Piece: reprint priority to TCG+ stores

**Finding:** On 2026-02-12 Bandai's English account announced OP-13 and EB-03 reprints distributing from around June 2026 with priority allocation to Bandai TCG+ stores that support Organized Play on a steady basis (paraphrase of the post). Premium Bandai USA also reopened chance-to-buy sales.
**Confidence:** sourced (text from search-index snippets of the official post; direct fetch returned HTTP 402)
**Source:** <https://x.com/ONEPIECE_tcg_EN/status/2021760732887662698>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

The rule rewards OP history. A store that opens in 2027 has none.

**Disconfirming check:** Bandai moved English sets toward simultaneous global release in 2026 (OP-16 English release 2026-06-12 per Bandai's product page). Several secondary sites claim this is meant to ease access; no Bandai primary statement retrieved.

### 3.5 Magic: The Gathering

**Finding:** ICv2's managing editor wrote that retailers often say "Wizards of the Coast's allocations for these products are brutal" for Universes Beyond sets; in Aug 2026 Thorne reported Play Boosters for The Hobbit arriving late to WPN stores and "not getting all of the product to WPN stores has been an ongoing problem for WotC for several releases."
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://icv2.com/articles/columns/view/60356/the-jds-perspective-magic-the-gathering-universes-beyond-dilemmas> (2025-08-21) ; <https://icv2.com/articles/columns/view/63071/rolling-initiative-the-hobbit-prerelease-shortages-r-i-p-michael-mirth> (2026-08-10)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Magic carries a different risk: when a set does not sell, the store holds the inventory. ICv2's editor warns newer shops "probably don't have much experience dealing with Magic sets that bomb."

**Disconfirming check:** Looked for allocation mechanics tied to WPN metrics. The 2019 WPN FAQ (<https://wpn.wizards.com/en/news/new-wpn-32-common-questions>) tied promo packs and advance product orders (2 to 26 cases) to tickets and engaged players. No current version found.

### 3.6 How long it takes to build allocation history

**Finding:** No publisher or distributor states a time to earn allocation. Every source that describes the mechanism ties it to past purchases (Southern Hobby: "based on past sales") or to reported organized play (Play! Pokémon, Bandai, Riftbound, WPN).
**Confidence:** estimate
**Source:** sections 1.1, 1.4, 2.1, 3.4 above
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Our inference: a new store's first 6 to 12 months of hot-product allocation will be small, because both inputs (purchase history, OP attendance) start at zero. The 6 to 12 months span is a `guess` with no source behind it. PHD's own credit-terms rule (6 months of buying history) is the only published time threshold found.

**Disconfirming check:** Looked for distributor statements on new-account allocation ramps. None published.

### 3.7 Distributor discount off MSRP

**Finding:** No source found gives a distributor-to-retailer discount for any TCG. The closest trade figure is "most distributors offer D&D products at a discount of 46-47%" (ICv2, 2023-10-08); PHD says discounts are "calculated based off of the SRP" and vary by manufacturer and product.
**Confidence:** sourced (proxy; different product category; older than 18 months)
**Source:** <https://icv2.com/articles/columns/view/55272/rolling-initiative-wizards-coasts-very-eventful-week> ; <https://www.phdgames.com/faqs/>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

46% is not a TCG figure and is not used as one. Search results offered TCG wholesale figures from vending and SEO sites; under the evidence rules on the overview page they are not recorded. Thorne (2023) wrote that online sellers price Magic booster boxes "$10 to $20 over cost," which implies thin cost-to-street spreads for boxes, but gives no cost.

**Disconfirming check:** MSRP is also soft: WotC's MSRPs are, in its own words, suggestions, and retailers "can price our products however they like" (<https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/magic-returns-to-listing-msrp-with-foundations>, 2024-10-25). A percentage off a soft MSRP says little until checked against street price.

## 4. What a store without allocation does, and what it costs

### 4.1 Options

Sources describe four routes: distributor allocation (sections 3.1 to 3.5), buying at big-box retail at MSRP, buying sealed on the secondary market (TCGplayer, eBay), and buying from customers (store-credit buylists, card shows, streamers' bulk; TCGplayer seller blog, 2025-04-29, <https://seller.tcgplayer.com/blog/4-ways-to-source-pok%C3%A9mon-inventory-when-distributors-are-sold-out>, no figures given). Buying from other stores for resale breaks GTS terms for Wizards product (section 2.2).

### 4.2 Big-box at MSRP: Pokémon ETB

**Finding:** Buying a Pokémon ETB at retail MSRP and paying Indiana sales tax costs $53.49; selling it at MSRP loses $3.50; selling at the Aug 2026 secondary price of $65 to $69 gives an 18% to 22% gross margin before card fees.
**Confidence:** estimate
**Source:** ETB MSRP $49.99 and secondary $65 to $69: <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/63244/pokemon-tcg-shows-signs-cooling-off-from-hot-summer-sales> (2026-08-28). Indiana sales tax 7%: <http://www.in.gov/dor/i-am-a/business-corp/sales-tax/>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Arithmetic: $49.99 x 1.07 = $53.49. At $49.99: $49.99 - $53.49 = -$3.50. At $65: ($65 - $53.49) / $65 = 17.7%. At $69: ($69 - $53.49) / $69 = 22.5%. Assumes the big-box store will not honor an Indiana resale exemption certificate (not verified either way). Purchase limits apply: ICv2 describes Sam's Club at two per person. The $65 to $69 secondary price had already fallen 23% to 28% from about $90 at launch.

**Disconfirming check:** If the 30th Celebration secondary price holds above $69, margin rises. The ICv2 data shows the direction for the prior two sets was down.

### 4.3 Worked example: One Piece OP-16 "The Time of Battle" booster box

**Owner's observation, 2026-09-30:** TCGplayer "The Time of Battle Booster Box" $223.82; "The Time of Battle Booster Box Case" $2,441.71.

**Identification**

| Item | Value | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Game and set | One Piece Card Game, BOOSTER PACK -THE TIME OF BATTLE- [OP-16] | <https://en.onepiece-cardgame.com/products/op16.html> | fact |
| English release | 2026-06-12 | same | fact |
| MSRP per pack | USD $4.99 | same | fact |
| Packs per box | 24 (12 cards each) | TCGplayer listing text, <https://mp-search-api.tcgplayer.com/v2/product/689336/details> | sourced |
| Boxes per case | 12 | TCGplayer case listing text, <https://mp-search-api.tcgplayer.com/v2/product/689338/details> | sourced |
| MSRP per box | $4.99 x 24 = $119.76 | arithmetic | estimate |

Bandai's product page gives only the per-pack price. It does not state packs per box or boxes per case; those come from TCGplayer's listing text. The publisher page does not give box count.

**TCGplayer data on 2026-09-30** (public product-details endpoint, the same figures the owner saw):

| Product | Market price | Lowest listing | Lowest incl. shipping | Listed median | Listings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Booster box (689336) | $223.82 | $70.00 (outlier, likely a mislisting) | $220.00 | $249.96 | 45 |
| Case of 12 (689338) | $2,441.71 | $2,659.00 | $2,683.98 | n/a | 14 |

TCGplayer's definition of "market price" was not retrieved; it is a figure derived from recent sales, not a price anyone is offering today.

**Cost per box and case discount** (confidence: estimate)

- Case at market price: $2,441.71 / 12 = **$203.48 per box**.
- Discount against a single box at market: ($223.82 - $203.48) / $223.82 = **9.1%**.
- Against MSRP: $203.48 / $119.76 = 1.70, so **70% above MSRP**.
- Case at the lowest live listing with shipping: $2,683.98 / 12 = **$223.67 per box**, which is **more** than the cheapest single box with shipping ($220.00). Today's actual case offers carry no bulk discount.
- If Indiana's 7% sales tax is charged on the purchase and not recovered: $203.48 x 1.07 = **$217.72 per box**. Whether TCGplayer accepts an Indiana resale exemption is not verified.

**Gross margin if sold in store** (cost basis $203.48 from case at market; before card processing, rent, labor)

| Shelf price | Price | Gross profit | Gross margin | Margin if tax paid on purchase ($217.72 cost) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TCGplayer market | $223.82 | $20.34 | 9.1% | 2.7% |
| Market +20% | $223.82 x 1.2 = $268.58 | $65.11 | 24.2% | 18.9% |
| Market +50% | $223.82 x 1.5 = $335.73 | $132.25 | 39.4% | 35.2% |

Arithmetic, tax-paid column: ($223.82 - $217.72) / $223.82 = 2.7%; ($268.58 - $217.72) / $268.58 = 18.9%; ($335.73 - $217.72) / $335.73 = 35.2%.

At the lowest live case listing ($223.67 per box), in-store margin is 0.1% at market, 16.7% at +20%, 33.4% at +50%.

Whether a walk-in buyer pays 20% or 50% over the price on their phone is a `guess`. No evidence on local price tolerance found. For scale: a Thorne ICv2 column uses a 45% retail margin as its worked example for a D&D book (<https://icv2.com/articles/columns/view/49117/rolling-initiative-extinction-level-event>); it is an illustration, not a survival threshold. Reaching about 40% on secondary-sourced OP-16 takes a $335.73 shelf price for a box TCGplayer sells for $223.82.

**Net if resold on TCGplayer** (Level 1 to 4 seller)

- Commission: 10.75% (effective 2026-02-10; cap $75 per item). Source: <https://seller.tcgplayer.com/blog/important-changes-to-tcgplayer-direct-minimum-pricing-and-marketplace-fees>. Confidence: fact.
- Transaction fee: 2.5% + $0.30 on the order total, which includes sales tax. Source: <https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/201357836-TCGplayer-Fees>, read through the help center JSON endpoint in [online-and-legal.md](online-and-legal.md) section 1.1. Confidence: fact.
- At $223.82 with 7% Indiana tax collected from the buyer: order total $223.82 x 1.07 = $239.49. Commission $223.82 x 0.1075 = $24.06 (below the $75 cap); transaction fee $239.49 x 0.025 = $5.99, + $0.30 = $6.29. Net = $223.82 - $24.06 - $6.29 = **$193.47**.
- Against the $203.48 case cost: $193.47 - $203.48 = **-$10.01 per box** (-4.9% of cost). Against the $217.72 tax-paid cost: $193.47 - $217.72 = **-$24.25 per box**. Both before postage, packaging and the seller's shipping charge, which were not modeled.

**At distributor pricing:** not computable from sourced data. No TCG distributor discount was found (section 3.7). For scale only, using the D&D proxy that does not transfer: $119.76 x (1 - 0.46) = $64.67 per box; sold at $223.82 that is a 71% gross margin. The gap between $64.67 and $203.48 is why allocation, not account access, decides sealed-product margin. The proxy shows the size of the lever; it is not a basis for any forecast.

**Disconfirming check:** A lower case price would change the answer. Today's live case listings are above market price, not below. OP-16 is also not a scarce set on current data (section 4.4), so waiting may lower the buy price, but it lowers the sell price by the same move.
