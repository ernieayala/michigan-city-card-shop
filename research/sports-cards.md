# Sports Cards: Licensing, Supply Access, Margins, and Risk

> Last updated: 2026-10-01

Scope: the sports-card side of a proposed card shop in Michigan City, Indiana (storefront or Indiana-based online-only). TCG distribution, TCG economics, local competition, marketplace fees, and storefront costs belong to other research files.

Retrieval note: every URL below was retrieved 2026-09-30. Where a page returned 403/404 or could not be parsed, the entry says "page not opened" and the figure comes from the search-engine excerpt. Treat those as one tier weaker than stated until someone opens the page.

## Headline (bad news first)

1. A new store cannot open a Topps direct account today. Fanatics Collect Pro states it is "not currently accepting applications from Breakers, Retail stores, or Hobby Shops for any of our direct account programs," and Topps now holds the MLB, NBA, and NFL licenses. The licensed product customers want most is the product a new store cannot buy at wholesale.
2. Every published direct program (Topps, Panini, Upper Deck) requires a physical storefront. An online-only store qualifies for none of them.
3. Panini requires 6 months of storefront operation and sports cards at 70% of gross sales. A mixed TCG and sports shop fails the 70% test if TCG is the larger line.
4. Hot licensed Topps product in 2025-2026 trades above MSRP, not below. The spread goes to whoever holds allocation. A store without allocation buys at the secondary price, which is above the price Topps charges consumers on its own site.
5. Sports-card grading volume fell 12% in 2025 while TCG grading rose 95% (GemRate via SI). The collector growth is on the TCG side.
6. Box breaks, the highest-margin sports-card activity, are the subject of a pending California qui tam suit and roughly 70 arbitration demands alleging illegal lottery. Topps's 2023 direct-account rules limited shops to in-store breaks.

## 2. How a store gets hobby product

### Topps / Fanatics direct

**Finding:** Fanatics Collect Pro is not accepting direct-account applications from breakers, retail stores, or hobby shops; accounts approved before 2026-01-13 proceed; "there is no alternative application process or route available."
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://www.fanaticscollectpro.com/users/apply> (page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

One exception exists: the "Topps Hobby Prospect Program" for "an existing Comic, TCG or Gaming shop that is open a minimum of 40 hours per week," reviewed monthly by the Topps Hobby Account Team, which "will reach out if your business is strong fit." The form asks for lease terms, POS and security systems, 2023-2024 sales by category, sourcing, social media follower counts, and community involvement (<https://www.fanaticscollectpro.com/users/apply/comic-shop>, page fetched). A new store has no sales history to report. No minimum order or product list is published.

**Disconfirming check:** Looked for a distributor route to Topps. GTS, Topps's leading distributor for 20+ years, lost Topps after 2022 as Fanatics moved to direct accounts (SCD, 2022-08-06, page fetched). Southern Hobby's homepage lists Panini, Upper Deck, and Leaf, and no Topps (<https://www.southernhobby.com/>, page fetched). Cardlines (2024-05-16, page fetched) reports a dealer told he could not buy from distributors because he held a Topps Direct account. No wholesale Topps route for a non-account store was found.

### Topps direct-account rules (for context if an account is ever granted)

**Finding:** Topps's June 2023 rules for its ~680 direct hobby shops barred online and B2B sales unless approved in writing, limited breaks to in-store with customers present, and required 7 hours/day 6 days or 8 hours/day 5 days.
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/fanatics-topps-card-shop-rules-guidelines-sports-cards-memorabilia-hobby-shops> (2023-07-05, page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

17 guidelines, quarterly sales and social-media reporting, DealernetB2B sales allowed after a 30-day hold. Topps added nearly 300 of the 680 shops in the prior 10 months. These rules are three years old; current terms unknown.

**Disconfirming check:** Whether these rules were loosened after 2023. Not found.

### Fanatics allocation tiers

**Finding:** Leaked Fanatics partner documents reported by Cardlines rank accounts by spend (Blowout Cards $49.7M, DA Card World $42.3M, Blez $17.1M) and tie some breaker allocation to breaking on Fanatics Live.
**Confidence:** sourced (trade blog describing leaked documents; documents not seen)
**Source:** <https://cardlines.com/what-we-found-out-from-the-fanatics-partner-system-documents-and-what-it-means-for-the-hobby/> (2026-02-16, page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Cardlines's statement that shops at $50K-$200K annual volume "cannot compete" is the writer's interpretation. Record as guess.

**Disconfirming check:** Fanatics's Josh Luber said in 2022 "Card shops are 1A and 1B" (SCD 2022-08-06). The 2026 application freeze contradicts that stated priority.

### Panini direct

**Finding:** Panini's 2026 direct application requires a storefront open at least 6 months, 40+ hours/week, and sports cards at 70% of gross sales; orders are full cases only, prepaid by credit card, all subject to allocation.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://assets.paniniamerica.net/checklist/2026+Direct+Application.pdf> (PDF read in full)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Pricing rules: "Wholesale MAPP pricing effective for 30 days"; internet retailers "may not sell below direct MAPP wholesale price for 30 days after release" and "must maintain a price 20% above direct cost for 21 days after product release date." No US-Canada cross-border selling for 180 days. New accounts reviewed quarterly. The form asks whether the store breaks product to sell singles online and what share of revenue that is.

**Disconfirming check:** The 70% sports rule is the obstacle for a mixed shop. Whether Panini enforces it after approval is unknown.

**Confidence:** sourced (undated; lists an old Panini Arlington, TX address, so it predates the Irving address on the 2026 form)
**Source:** <https://www.gtsdistribution.com/images/Panini-Brick--Mortar-Application.pdf> (PDF read in full)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

### Upper Deck

**Finding:** Upper Deck Certified Diamond Dealers must own a brick-and-mortar storefront open 35 hours/week and may not sell sealed hobby product online for the first 180 days after release; applications go through an authorized distributor.
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://upperdeck.com/cdd-inquiry-distributor-locator/> (page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Authorized US distributors: GTS (East branch in Kentwood, MI, 800-940-0934), Southern Hobby (includes an Illinois branch), Universal Distribution, Magazine Exchange. Search excerpt of Upper Deck's CDD page: "no minimum order nor exclusivity requirements" (<https://upperdeckstore.com/become-a-certified-diamond-dealer>, not opened). Authorized Internet Retailers must already be CDDs (<https://upperdeck.com/authorized-internet-retailers/>, page fetched), so there is no online-only route.

### Distributor access for new accounts

**Finding:** Southern Hobby states "New customers will not have access to new release sports products, as allocations will be based on past sales," and its account survey requires the applicant to confirm that "all Sports Cards are allocated to existing customers based on past sales and there is no availability to new customers."
**Confidence:** fact
**Source:** <https://www.southernhobby.com/new_account.php> (page fetched; the quotes were confirmed by a second, independent fetch the same day)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

This closes one of the four Upper Deck distributors, and the Panini distributor route through Southern Hobby, to a new store. GTS, Universal and Magazine Exchange were not checked for an equivalent rule. PHD sets a $500 minimum on sports-card orders against $400 for gaming (<https://www.phdgames.com/faqs/>, fetched).

**Disconfirming check:** A distributor that sells new-release sports product to new accounts would reopen the Upper Deck route. Call GTS East (800-940-0934) and ask.

### Can online-only or brand-new stores get allocation?

**Finding:** No published program admits an online-only seller; a brand-new storefront qualifies for Upper Deck's CDD program from opening day but needs a distributor that will supply a new account (Southern Hobby will not), Panini after 6 months (if 70% sports), and Topps not at all under the current freeze.
**Confidence:** estimate (derived from the three fact entries above)
**Source:** the FC Pro, Panini 2026 PDF, and Upper Deck CDD pages cited above
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Hot-release allocation for new accounts (Bowman Chrome, Topps Chrome, Prizm): no primary data found. Panini says "All orders are subject to allocation." Topps sold 2025 Topps Chrome Football through a lottery-style launch system per search excerpts (not verified). Assume a new account receives a small share of hot releases and full orders of slow ones. That is a guess.

**Disconfirming check:** A licensor could reopen Topps applications. The FC Pro page gives no reopening date.

### Hot licensed product trades above MSRP in 2025-2026

**Finding:** 2025-26 Topps basketball hobby boxes ($109.99 MSRP) peaked at $225 and jumbos ($229.99) at $450 on the secondary market after the October 2025 preorder.
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://sports.yahoo.com/article/prices-2025-26-topps-basketball-190000883.html> (2025-10-03, Terapeak data, page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Other data points, search excerpts only: 2025-26 Topps Chrome Basketball hobby ($369.99) flipped up to $580 on eBay (cllct, page 404); 2025 Topps Chrome Football hobby (~$300 retail) at $560-$660 in September 2026 (excerpt, source unclear); 2026 Topps Chrome Baseball hobby ($239.99) listed at $474.95-$649.95 by online retailers (excerpt). 2026 Topps Series 1 hobby: Topps presale $99.99, DA Card World $209.95 (excerpt from <https://www.dacardworld.com/sports-cards/2026-topps-series-1-baseball-hobby-box>, 403 on fetch). A separate search summary attributed the same $209.95 to Blowout Cards ([storefront-costs-and-demand.md](storefront-costs-and-demand.md), section 8); neither page was opened.

**Disconfirming check:** A common assumption is that hobby boxes trade near or below distributor cost. For licensed Topps product in 2025-2026 the evidence runs the other way. Below-cost evidence was found for unlicensed Panini (next entry).

### Estimate: cost of buying Topps without an account

**Finding:** A store without a Topps account that buys 2026 Topps Series 1 hobby at $209.95 pays 110% more than the $99.99 Topps charged consumers at presale.
**Confidence:** estimate
**Source:** $99.99 from <https://www.checklistinsider.com/2026-topps-series-1-baseball> (fetched); $209.95 from DA Card World excerpt (page 403)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Arithmetic: ($209.95 - $99.99) / $99.99 = 1.10. The store's shelf price must exceed $209.95 to make any margin, while a customer who wins a Topps.com drop pays $99.99. The store sells to customers who missed the drop, at a thin markup over a secondary-market cost.

### Unlicensed Panini trades below retail

**Finding:** 2025-26 Panini Donruss Basketball (unlicensed) hobby boxes sold at an average of $190 each (two-box lot, $381).
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://www.si.com/collectibles/look-how-panini-first-unlicensed-basketball-cards-selling> (2026-06-13, page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Search excerpts put Donruss Basketball hobby retail at $309.95 and 2025-26 Prizm Basketball presell at $459.95 (Prizm presell confirmed on <https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/2025-26-panini-prizm-basketball-081242722.html>, fetched) with eBay sales at $257.88-$259.99 (excerpt, not verified).

Estimate, conditional on the unverified $309.95 retail: if Panini direct cost sat at the MAPP floor implied by the 20% rule, cost = $309.95 / 1.2 = $258.29, and a $190 sale is 26% below cost. The direct price itself was not found.

**Disconfirming check:** Signature Series (unlicensed) held value better per the same SI article. Not every unlicensed product falls below cost.

## 4. Market health

### 2021 peak and 2022-2023 correction

**Finding:** Card Ladder's CL50 index rose 337% from 2019 to March 2021, then fell 23% in 2022 and 9% in 2023.
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/collectibles-are-crashing-whiskey-rolexes-trading-cards/468532> (2024-01-19, page fetched; figures originate with Business Insider citing Card Ladder)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

CL50 tracks 49 star rookie cards and one Pokemon Charizard. It measures high-end singles, not sealed wax or shop revenue. Altan Insights: "Yes, the bubbles have popped."

### 2025 recovery in high-end singles

**Finding:** Card Ladder's Basketball Index rose 29% and Baseball Index 17% in 2025 through mid-September; collectors spent over $400M on cards online in August 2025, the most Card Ladder had tracked in a month.
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://sports.yahoo.com/article/move-over-baseball-basketball-cards-203000093.html> (cllct via Yahoo, 2025-09-19, page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

High-end singles recovering does not mean sealed product at a small shop is profitable. The record sale was a $12.93M Jordan/Kobe card.

**Disconfirming check:** 2026 year-to-date index data: Athlon's 2026 mid-year report returned 403. Not verified.

### Grading volume: sports flat or falling, TCG growing

**Finding:** Graders processed 26.8M cards in 2025 (+32% per the source; 26.8 / 20 = +34% on the rounded 2024 figure of 20M), but sports-card grading fell 12% industry-wide while TCG/non-sport grading rose 95%.
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://www.si.com/collectibles/over-26-million-cards-graded-in-2025-how-the-market-exploded> (2026-01-04, GemRate data, page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

PSA graded 19.26M (71.8% share); PSA sports grading up 2%. TCG graded at about 2:1 vs sports.

**Contradicting sources:** cllct reports 26.6M total (<https://www.cllct.com/sports-collectibles/sports-cards/major-authenticators-graded-more-than-26-million-cards-in-2025>, not opened; now returns HTTP 404).

## 5. Box breaks

### Business model

Breaks sell slots (teams or random assignments) in a sealed box or case opened on camera or in store; the breaker keeps the gap between slot revenue and box cost, less platform fees. No primary source for break margins was found. Any margin figure below is a guess.

Constraints found: Topps's 2023 direct rules allowed breaks "for in-store only where customers are present. No selling products for online-only breaks" (SCD 2023-07-05). Cardlines (2026-02-16) reports allocation favoring breakers who stream on Fanatics Live. Panini's direct form asks what share of revenue comes from breaking for online singles.

### Lottery litigation

**Finding:** A qui tam suit under the California False Claims Act filed July 2025 and later unsealed names Whatnot and Fanatics Live, alleging "unlicensed box-break lotteries"; the California DOJ declined to intervene; separately about 70 Whatnot customers filed arbitration demands in 2026; no adjudicated finding of illegality exists.
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://natlawreview.com/article/future-card-breaking-and-repacks-insights-california-litigation> (Womble Bond Dickinson, 2026-09-30, page fetched); <https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/us/insights/alerts/chase-trading-card-repacks-face-growing-legal-scrutiny> (2026-08-05, page fetched); <https://www.espn.com/mlb/story/_/id/49098946/could-box-breaking-considered-gambling-lottery> (page fetched, date not captured)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

The qui tam complaint includes 18 named plaintiffs in at least 11 states with break spending from a few hundred dollars to over $4M (ESPN). Claims: California Penal Code 319 lottery ban, RICO, consumer protection. California Penal Code 319.3 addresses "sports trading card grab bags" (repacks of cards removed from manufacturer packaging). Case name and number were not found.

**Disconfirming check (precedent favoring the industry):** Chaset v. Fleer/Skybox International, 300 F.3d 1083 (9th Cir. 2002-08-20) affirmed dismissal of RICO gambling claims over chase cards in factory packs: purchasers got the chance they paid for, so no RICO injury (<https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-9th-circuit/1167864.html>, not opened; holding from excerpts). That case covered factory packs, not breaks, and turned on RICO standing, not on whether packs are lotteries under state law.

### Indiana law

**Finding:** Indiana defines gambling as "risking money or other property for gain, contingent in whole or in part upon lot, chance, or the operation of a gambling device," excluding "bona fide business transactions that are valid under the law of contracts."
**Confidence:** fact (statute text via FindLaw)
**Source:** <https://codes.findlaw.com/in/title-35-criminal-law-and-procedure/in-code-sect-35-45-5-1.html> (page fetched; iga.in.gov returned no text)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

A random-team break (pay money, chance decides which team you get, the prize is the cards) fits the three elements on its face. Whether "bona fide business transaction" covers it is untested in Indiana as far as found.

**Finding:** Indiana HB 1052, effective 2026-07-01, bans online dual-currency sweepstakes games with civil penalties up to $100,000 per violation, enforced by the Indiana Gaming Commission; the law review summary does not mention trading cards, mystery boxes, or breaks.
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://natlawreview.com/article/sweepstakes-gaming-banned-indiana> (2026-03-21, page fetched)
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

No Indiana Gaming Commission guidance on card breaks was found. A search excerpt says single-currency card mystery-box sites (Card Crush, ZumbaCards) operate in Indiana outside HB 1052; not verified.

**Disconfirming check:** Looked for any Indiana enforcement against breakers. None found. Absence of enforcement is not a legal opinion.

## Implications for storefront vs online-only

- Online-only: shut out of Topps, Panini, and Upper Deck direct programs by their own published terms. Sealed sports product would come from secondary wholesalers at or above market price. Viable sports lines online are singles, consignment, and breaks, each with no verified margin and breaks carrying legal risk.
- Storefront, new: Upper Deck (hockey) CDD eligibility from day one, but Southern Hobby gives new customers no new-release sports product; GTS, Universal and Magazine Exchange are unchecked. Panini available after 6 months if sports cards are 70% of gross, which conflicts with a TCG-led mix. Topps unavailable under the current freeze; the Hobby Prospect Program for TCG/gaming shops is the only door and requires an operating history.
- Upper Deck bars online sales of sealed hobby for 180 days after release; Panini requires approval for online sales and a 20% markup floor for 21 days; Topps (2023 rules) barred online sales and online-only breaks. A storefront account limits online selling of the same product.
- The planning case: sports cards are a secondary line in a TCG-led store for at least the first 6-12 months, stocked with Upper Deck hockey if a distributor will supply a new account, secondary-market Topps at thin margin, and singles. A sports-first store fails on supply before it reaches demand.
- The single assumption that flips this: Topps reopening direct applications to new shops. If that happens and a new Michigan City account gets meaningful Chrome and Bowman allocation, the sealed-sports line becomes viable because hot product trades above MSRP. Nothing found suggests a reopening date.
