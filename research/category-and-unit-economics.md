# TCG Category Health and Small Game Store Unit Economics

> Last updated: 2026-10-02

## Summary

US and Canada hobby game sales reached $3.66B in 2025, up 29% to 32% after two
flat years, and trading card games are about two thirds of it. Part of that is
speculation: Lorcana sales fell once investment buyers left, and Special
Illustration singles from Pokémon's 30th Celebration set fell 40% to 50% soon
after release. The only store margins found come from one store owner: 27% to
35% on sealed product and 45% to 50% on singles. November and December carry
about 27% of yearly sales in the closest national series. No card-store failure
rate exists; all retail survives at about 60% after five years.

Scope: desk research only. No store owner, distributor, or publisher has been contacted. Every
figure below was retrieved 2026-09-30 unless marked otherwise. Several primary PDFs (Konami, Bandai
Namco, Hasbro press release) could not be parsed or timed out; each gap is stated where it applies.

## Headline

The category is at a demand peak, and part of that peak is speculative buying. Publisher revenue
grew 29% to 62% in 2025 and kept growing into mid-2026. The same period produced the first clean
bust signals: Lorcana sales fell after "investment" buyers left (Ravensburger, January 2026), and
Pokémon TCG: 30th Celebration singles lost 40% to 80% of launch value within a week (ICv2,
2026-09-23). A new store opening in 2027 would buy into a channel that ICv2's own white paper
flags as uncertain for 2027, with no allocation history, on sealed product that carries a 16% to
35% margin in the anecdotal evidence found.

## 1. How big is the hobby games channel, and how fast is it growing?

**Finding:** ICv2 estimates US and Canada hobby games retail sales at $3.66 billion in 2025, up from $2.84 billion in 2024, after two flat years (2023: +1%, 2024: about 0%).

**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Verbatim 2025 quote: "The hobby games market grew 40% to $3.66 billion in 2025 according to ICv2 estimates." ICv2 also states "TCGs now account for roughly 2/3 of hobby games dollars" and "The store count in the channel was definitely up."

**Arithmetic check (estimate):** $3.66B / $2.84B = 1.289, a 28.9% increase, not 40%. Either ICv2 restated 2024 down to about $2.61B, or the 40% refers to a different base. The ICv2 GAMA Expo white paper gives hobby channel growth of 32% for 2025. Use 29% to 32% until the ICv2 Pro report resolves it.

**TCG slice (estimate):** 2/3 x $3.66B = about $2.44B in 2025. The white paper says TCG sales rose "roughly $1 billion", which implies about $1.44B in 2024, or 51% of $2.84B. That matches ICv2's 2024 wording that TCGs were "over half" of hobby dollars.

**Channel growth history (ICv2 white paper, sourced):** 2020 +15%, 2021 +30%, 2022 +10%, 2023 +2%, 2024 +2%, 2025 +32%. Source: <https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/icv2-hobby-games-white-paper-2025-gama-expo-2026/286373737> (ICv2 deck posted to SlideShare; ICv2's own page for it not located).

**Disconfirming check:** Would the growth vanish without its lead brands? Two brands carry it. The white paper attributes 2025 to Magic ("up over 50%") and Pokémon, and 2023 to 2024 were flat with collectible games declining in 2024. Two flat years sat between the Covid spike and the 2025 spike. The channel has not shown steady compounding; it has shown step changes driven by hit products.

**Contradicting sources:** Internal ICv2 inconsistency on the 2025 growth rate (40% vs 32% vs 28.9% by arithmetic). The white paper's 2023 +2% and 2024 +2% also differ from the ICv2 articles' +1% and about 0% for the same years. The ICv2 Pro article "TCGs Had Hockey Stick Growth in 2025" (<https://icv2.com/articles/icv2-pro/view/62103/tcgs-had-hockey-stick-growth-2025>) is paywalled; its figures were not retrieved.

## 2. Is 2024-2026 a speculative peak?

**Finding:** Yes, in part. ICv2 reports that "speculation is a significant contributor to sales" in 2026, and two brands have shown post-hype declines in the last nine months.
**Confidence:** sourced
**Source:** <https://icv2.com/articles/icv2-pro/view/62103/tcgs-had-hockey-stick-growth-2025> (paywalled; the speculation sentence is in the public preview); <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/61527/disney-lorcana-sales-declined-2025>; <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/63449/pokmon-tcg-flippers-meltdown-discord-wave-schadenfreude-washes-over-some-fans>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Evidence for a speculative component:

1. **Lorcana, 2025 (sourced).** Ravensburger: "Those joining the brand with a primary interest in investment have since withdrawn." Group sales fell 5.9%. A publisher saying this about its own product in an annual report is the strongest bust signal found.
2. **Pokémon TCG: 30th Celebration, September 2026 (sourced).** ICv2, 2026-09-23: Special Illustration singles down 40% to 50%, a rare Pikachu illustration down over 80%, attributed to higher pull rates and oversupply. A flipper quoted as holding "over 2000" ETBs. ICv2 notes "local stores are not accepting returns."
3. **Hobby stores shut out of the hot product (sourced, single-store anecdote).** Scott Thorne, owner of Castle Perilous Games & Books, Carbondale IL, ICv2 column 2026-09-28: one distributor allocated his store "one ETB; not one case of ETBs, only a single one ETB" of 30th Celebration while Target and Sam's Club drew lines. <https://icv2.com/articles/columns/view/63479/rolling-initiative-glut-pokmon-tcg-30th-celebration-not-hobby-retail>
4. **Theft as a cost of scarcity (sourced).** ICv2 reports card-store burglaries in September 2026: MadRat Toys, Houston ($10,000 to $15,000 merchandise, about $10,000 damage); Nakama Collectibles, Cypress TX (store 5 months old); Branson Dragon Game Store, MO (about $10,000 merchandise, $1,000 cash). <https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/63498/rash-trading-card-thefts-continues-d-d-themed-restaurant-next-game-store-visit-biggest-game-store-waco>
5. **ICv2 white paper outlook (sourced):** "TCG expansion anticipated but uncertainty for 2027."

**Sports-card analog, 2021-2023:**
- Sports Collectors Digest, 2022-09-23: Card Ladder modern (1984-2008) index down 20.8% year over year; vintage up 13.64%. <https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/sports-card-market-values-vintage-cards-modern-pwcc-card-ladder> (sourced).
- Card Ladder CL50 down 23% in 2022 and 9% in 2023 (search summary of <https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/sports-card-market-report-2022-23/>, which returned HTTP 403). NOT VERIFIED. If correct, cumulative decline is 1 - (0.77 x 0.91) = 30% (estimate).
- Fanatics bought Topps (January 2022) and moved toward direct-to-consumer sales; Sports Collectors Digest reported shop owners' concern about being cut out (<https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/sports-card-shops-fanatics-reaction-topps-panini>, not fetched). This is a second risk beyond price: the licensor bypassing the shop.

**Magic, 2023 (guess, anecdotal).** Gary Ray, Black Diamond Games, Concord CA, 2023-10-08: "Commander Masters saw its value plummet days after release"; market price fell "below cost that quickly"; he held $12K of it at "a dismal 16% margin" and $8K of other dead Magic. <http://blackdiamondgames.blogspot.com/2023/10/magic-price-collapse.html>

**What happened to shops that opened in the 2020-2021 boom:** no store-level closure data found. The nearest proxy is BLS Business Employment Dynamics for all retail trade (NAICS 44-45), <https://www.bls.gov/bdm/us_age_naics_44_table7.txt> (sourced; fetched through WebFetch because direct download returned BLS "Access Denied"):

| Cohort (openings in year ended March) | Surviving 1 yr | 2 yr | 3 yr | 4 yr | 5 yr |
|------|------|------|------|------|------|
| 2020 | 88.0% | 79.6% | 72.3% | 65.9% | 59.8% |
| 2021 (boom year) | 83.6% | 74.7% | 67.4% | 60.8% | n/a |
| 2022 | 87.8% | 77.6% | 68.5% | n/a | n/a |

The 2021 retail cohort lost 16.4% of establishments in year one, worse than the 2020 (12.0%) and 2022 (12.2%) cohorts. That is consistent with boom-year openings failing faster, but it covers all retail, not card shops. The 2015 retail cohort had 44.2% surviving at 10 years.

**Disconfirming check:** What would show this is not a bubble? In-store play growing independent of sealed resale. ICv2's 2026 preview says "in-store play was also up substantially during 2026." Circana says 19% of adults bought trading cards "for collecting, resale, and gameplay" (Q1 2025, via search summary of <https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/2025/06/06/collectible-trading-cards-and-adults-drive-toy-sales-in-2025-circana>, not fetched). The play base is real. The open question is what share of 2025-2026 dollars it accounts for. No source found splits play demand from resale demand.

**Contradicting sources:** Hasbro raised 2026 guidance in July 2026 on Magic strength (8-K above). Ravensburger says Lorcana's player base keeps growing even as sales fell. Neither refutes a speculative component; both say the core is larger than zero.

## 3. What margins does a small game store earn, by revenue line?

**Finding:** No survey-grade margin data was found. Anecdotal owner data puts sealed TCG at 16% to 35% gross margin, singles at 45% to 50%, and a whole-store target near 45%.
**Confidence:** guess (anecdotal; one experienced owner and one 2015 column)
**Source:** <http://blackdiamondgames.blogspot.com/2023/02/magic-box-prices.html> ; <http://blackdiamondgames.blogspot.com/2021/12/why-magic-singles-are-good-for-game.html> ; <http://blackdiamondgames.blogspot.com/2021/12/why-magic-singles-are-bad-for-game.html> ; <http://blackdiamondgames.blogspot.com/2018/09/rising-cost-of-goods-tradecraft.html>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

Gary Ray has run Black Diamond Games (Concord CA) since about 2004 and wrote *Friendly Local Game Store: A Five-Year Path to a Middle-Class Income*. He is the most-cited retail voice in the channel; his numbers are one store's.

- **Sealed Magic (2023-02-08):** wholesale cost $91.12 for a draft booster box (other distributors $92.70, $95.16); sold at $125, matching TCGplayer median. Gross margin (125 - 91.12) / 125 = 27.1% (estimate, matches his stated 27%). Keystone would be about $182 to $190; the market will not bear it.
- **Sealed Magic (2021-12):** "30-35%, in a trade that regularly gets 40-45%."
- **Singles (2021-12):** "we were getting around a 45-50% margin." Costs: labor at "$15+ an hour" to sort; online marketplace fees "up to 15%"; "$50K sounds like a reasonable start" to rebuild a singles case.
- **Whole store:** target "around 45%"; achieved 47% in 2022. CCGs were "about a third" of a projected "couple million dollars" in annual sales.
- **Turn thresholds (2018-09):** "50% margin needs to turn at four times a year, 45% turn at five times a year, 40% six times, and anything lower perhaps ten times a year."
- **Accessories:** Ultra Pro has "laughably low margins, if you use their MSRP." No wholesale discount figure found.

**Pokémon sealed wholesale:** no primary source found. Pages claiming "about 50% off MSRP" at authorized distributors are vending-machine and sourcing blogs, which the evidence rules on the overview page exclude; not cited. Unknown.

**Magic MSRP (sourced):** Wizards restored MSRPs in October 2024: Foundations Play Booster $5.25, Collector Booster $24.99, Bundle $49.99 (<https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/58119/wotc-returns-msrps-magic-the-gathering>). Reality Fracture Play Booster $5.49, 14 cards (ICv2, 2026-09-25). A 30-pack display at $5.49 is $164.70 at MSRP (estimate). Wizards: "retailers can price our products however they like." Online sellers price below MSRP; a store's realized price follows TCGplayer, not MSRP.

**Revenue mix:** no current survey found. GAMA ran its first Tabletop Game Retailer Survey in August 2024 (<https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/57477/gama-announces-their-first-tabletop-game-survey>); no public copy of the results was found. The only mix data found: Marcus King, Troll and Toad (a large online seller, London KY), ICv2 2015-08-26: Magic sales shifted from 20% sealed boxes / 50% loose packs / 30% singles toward 15% / 30% / singles "nearly doubling." Stale (11 years) and from an online-heavy seller; not usable for a local store.

**Events:** no event-margin data found. A search summary credits Gary Ray with the claim that a play space on one third of the floor raised sales 60%. The originating post was not retrieved. NOT VERIFIED. Stores pay out entry fees as prize product valued at cost, so any event margin comes from the prize-support spread and from attendee purchases (guess).

**Disconfirming check:** Would a store with a higher sealed margin contradict this? A store that holds allocated hot product can sell above MSRP during a shortage; ICv2's retailer quote "there's money to be made if you've got them" describes that. A new store will not have the allocation (section 5), so it faces the commodity-priced sealed margin, not the shortage margin.

**Contradicting sources:** none found with data.

The national data shows the weakest month at 0.72x average for this category, a smaller swing than a lakefront dessert shop would face, but the data are not card-store specific. Release timing is also lumpy: a quarter with a hit set can outsell a quarter without one by a wide margin (Magic passed $500M in Q2 2026, per the Hasbro 8-K), so month-to-month variance comes from the publisher calendar rather than weather.

## 5. How much working capital does inventory tie up, and how does sealed product lose value?

**Finding:** Sealed product can fall below wholesale cost within days of release, and a new store gets the smallest allocations of hot product while carrying full exposure to overprinted product.
**Confidence:** guess for the dollar amounts (anecdotal); sourced for the allocation and price-drop events
**Source:** <http://blackdiamondgames.blogspot.com/2023/10/magic-price-collapse.html> ; <http://blackdiamondgames.blogspot.com/2023/02/magic-box-prices.html> ; <https://icv2.com/articles/columns/view/63479/rolling-initiative-glut-pokmon-tcg-30th-celebration-not-hobby-retail>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

- Commander Masters (2023): market price fell below Black Diamond's cost "days after release"; $12K held at 16% margin, and $8K of dead Magic from the prior three years. Earlier, "a Corolla worth of COVID era dead Magic" (2023-02-08). (guess, anecdotal)
- Pokémon 30th Celebration (2026): mass retail got the volume; one hobby store got one ETB from one distributor (sourced).
- Allocation rules for new accounts: Southern Hobby's written terms say "allocations will be based on past sales" ([distribution-and-supply.md](distribution-and-supply.md), section 2.1, fact). No distributor states a ramp or time threshold for new accounts. The claim that distributors require 6+ months of history and prioritize accounts that buy evergreen product comes from sourcing blogs; not cited. A call to a distributor (GTS, Southern Hobby, ACD, Universal (formerly Alliance)) would settle it.
- Magic WPN Premium status requires 1,500 tickets, 30 engaged players, and 10 activated players per annual check (<https://wpn.wizards.com/en/premium-hub>, retrieved in [distribution-and-supply.md](distribution-and-supply.md), section 1.3, fact).

**Inventory sizing (estimate built on guesses):** At Gary Ray's 45% margin and 5 turns per year, inventory at cost = annual revenue x 0.55 / 5 = 11% of annual revenue. A store doing $400,000 per year (guess, no source) would carry about $44,000 at cost, and about $50,000 more to seed a singles case (Gary Ray 2021, anecdotal). Sealed-heavy mixes at 27% to 35% margin need 6 to 10 turns by his thresholds, which a new store with thin allocations is unlikely to reach. Replace the $400,000 with a sourced revenue estimate before this is used in a forecast.

**Disconfirming check:** Does sealed ever appreciate? Yes, for sets that go out of print scarce, which is how the speculative buyer base formed. The 2023 Magic and 2026 Pokémon cases show the reverse happens when publishers print to demand. Pokémon's reported move to print at capacity (not verified) points toward more sets behaving like 30th Celebration, not fewer.

**Contradicting sources:** none with data.

## 6. What is the failure rate for hobby game stores?

**Finding:** No source reports a failure rate for hobby game or card stores; the best available proxy is BLS all-retail survival, about 60% at 4 to 5 years for the 2020 and 2021 cohorts.
**Confidence:** sourced (proxy)
**Source:** <https://www.bls.gov/bdm/us_age_naics_44_table7.txt>
**Retrieved:** 2026-09-30

A figure of "around 75% [of game stores] failing" within 2 to 3 years, appeared in a search snippet from therewillbe.games ("Why Game Stores Fail"). The page returned HTTP 404 and the snippet itself says "there aren't exactly a lot of studies." Recorded as guess, unverified, not usable. A second post, ccgprime.com "Game Store Life #2: The odds are against you", also returned HTTP 404.

ICv2 reports hobby store counts "flat to up" in 2023 and 2024 and "definitely up" in 2025. Net count growth says nothing about the closure rate: openings can outrun closures in a boom year. ICv2's closure coverage found in this research is individual (AMIGO Games distributor; GameStop's corporate closures), not a count.

**Disconfirming check:** If card stores survived better than general retail, industry counts would show it. No card-store establishment count was obtained: Census County Business Patterns requires an API key (NAICS 451120 / 459120 mixes hobby, toy, and game stores regardless).

**Contradicting sources:** none.
