## Summary

Michigan City has no card store on the Wizards, Ravensburger or Konami store
lists; the nearest three are 11 miles away in Chesterton and La Porte. Card
sales are at record levels, card shows sell out, and Franklin Street retail
space asks $6.50 a square foot a year.

Two co-owners propose a card store that buys collections and sells singles at
market prices, runs events most nights, and sells online. It starts at card
shows and online for $11,775 to $21,775, with no lease. It opens an 858 sq ft
store on Franklin Street for $39,633 to $44,037 once a year of show and online
sales has tested demand. Fixed costs are $1,563 a month, so the store breaks
even at about $6,400 a month in sales.

Both stages need about $46,400 to $59,600, from the owners and a loan. Sales
scenarios run from $3,842 to $13,448 a month; stage 1's sales log shows which
one holds before a lease is signed.

Figures carry the confidence tiers defined on the [overview](README.md):
`fact`, `sourced`, `estimate`, `guess`.

## 2. The business

| Part | Plan |
|------|------|
| What drives profit | Buying collections at posted rates and selling singles; sealed product bought to event demand and sold through, not held |
| Sales mix | 55% singles, 30% sealed; about 76% trading card games and 24% sports cards |
| Games | Magic, One Piece and Pokémon first; Yu-Gi-Oh! and Riftbound second; others on demand |
| Sports cards | Singles, graded rookies and vintage bought from the public; a few licensed sealed boxes |
| Channels | Over the counter, card shows, TCGplayer, eBay, and a weekly eBay Live or Whatnot stream |
| Events | Tuesday to Sunday, 20 seats |
| Fair dealing | Posted buy prices for every seller; singles at market or below; hot releases at a posted price near list, players first; no repacks, mystery boxes or breaks |

Details are in the [storefront plan](storefront-plan.md), sections 1 and 7.

### Sales scenarios

No store's sales figures were available, so the scenarios rest on the one
local anchor: the area's estimated trading card game spending, $6.37 a person
across 47,771 residents, about $304,000 a year or $25,358 a month
([storefront plan](storefront-plan.md), section 5, guess). Each scenario sets
the share of that spending the store's in-store trading card game lines (66% of
its sales) take; sports and online lines follow the plan's mix. The shares are
guesses, and stage 1 replaces them.

| Scenario | Share of area spending | Monthly sales | Monthly profit | Yearly sales | Yearly profit |
|----------|-----------------------:|--------------:|---------------:|-------------:|--------------:|
| Low | 10% | $3,842 | -$627 | $46,106 | -$7,526 |
| Base | 20% | $7,684 | $309 | $92,213 | $3,704 |
| High | 35% | $13,448 | $1,712 | $161,372 | $20,550 |
| Break-even | 16.7% | $6,417 | $0 | $77,004 | $0 |
| Plan target | 48.8% | $18,735 | $3,000 | $224,820 | $36,000 |

The shares leave out buyers from outside the area, sports spending beyond the
plan's mix, and online sales beyond 17% of the total. The plan target needs
those.

**First three years** (year 1 averages 75% of the steady level while the store
builds its player base, guess; years 2 and 3 hold steady, and growth beyond
that is stage 3):

| Scenario | Year 1 sales | Year 1 profit | Years 2 and 3, sales each | Years 2 and 3, profit each |
|----------|-------------:|--------------:|--------------------------:|---------------------------:|
| Low | $34,580 | -$10,333 | $46,106 | -$7,526 |
| Base | $69,159 | -$1,911 | $92,213 | $3,704 |
| High | $121,029 | $10,723 | $161,372 | $20,550 |

The operating cash, $4,404 to $8,808, covers the low scenario's losses for 7
to 14 months, past the month-9 review.

### Owner pay

The scenarios pay the owners nothing. Priced at the median wage for retail
supervisors in the Michigan City-La Porte area, $21.75 an hour (BLS, May 2025,
fact), the owner's 55 hours a week and the partner's 25 (guess) are worth
$7,540 a month. Covering that as well takes $37,373 a month in sales, close to
all of the area's estimated trading card game spending. The owners do not need
income from the store; profit goes back into stock until they choose otherwise.

**Loan payments and break-even** (11% a year over 5 years, guess; a lender
quote replaces it). Payments include principal, so the break-even is on cash:

| Loan | Monthly payment | Break-even sales | Share of area spending |
|-----:|----------------:|-----------------:|-----------------------:|
| $20,000 | $435 | $8,202 | 21.3% |
| $30,000 | $652 | $9,095 | 23.7% |
| $40,000 | $870 | $9,988 | 26.0% |

The base scenario does not cover any of these payments; the high scenario
covers all three. The loan therefore waits for stage 1: the store borrows once
stage 1 sales point to the loan's break-even or better, and a smaller loan sets
a lower bar. Lenders to a new LLC commonly ask the owners for a personal
guarantee (general knowledge).

## 8. Risks

| Risk | What the plan does |
|------|--------------------|
| Sales below break-even | Stage 1 tests demand before a lease; fixed costs of $1,563 a month; operating cash for 7 to 14 months of the low scenario; month-9 review |
| The card market is at a peak with a speculative share ([category](research/category-and-unit-economics.md), section 2) | Sealed product bought to event demand and sold through, not held; chase singles bought after a set's first weeks |
| New stores get little hot product in year one | Singles carry the margin; One Piece is the only deep sealed line |
| Collections cost more than planned | Tiered, posted buy rates; store credit offers; high-value cards sold in store and at shows |
| Players keep driving to Chesterton and La Porte | Events Tuesday to Sunday in town; stage 1 counts local players at nearby events |
| Theft | Two people on event nights; high-value stock in the safe overnight; collectibles covered by insurance |
| Lease and loan commitments | Signed only after stage 1; personal guarantee capped in the lease |

The full risk list is in the [storefront plan](storefront-plan.md), section 9.
