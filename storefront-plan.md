# Storefront Plan

> Last updated: 2026-10-05
> Status: draft built from the seven research files and the owners' choices.
> Quotes and local counts come next (section 2).

## Summary

A card store on Franklin Street run by two co-owners: the owner full time and
a partner on event nights, with no employees. It makes most of its money
buying collections and selling singles, over the counter, at card shows and
online, and it runs events most nights. The mix follows profit rather than a
fixed split: singles are 55% of sales, sealed product 30%, and trading card
games about three quarters of the total, sports cards a quarter. It deals
fairly: posted buy prices, market-price singles, no repacks or mystery boxes
(section 1). A Wyoming parent LLC owns the Indiana LLC that runs the store.

It starts small. Stage 1, buying and selling at card shows and online, costs
$11,775 to $21,775. Stage 2, an 858 sq ft store with 20 seats, costs $39,633 to
$44,037. The store breaks even at about $6,400 a month in sales, about 21% of
the area's estimated card spending, and profit goes back into stock until the
owners choose to take any out.

Plan figures are estimates built on guesses unless a table marks them
otherwise; confidence tiers are defined on the [overview](README.md). The
market evidence behind the mix is in
[what sells in 2026](research/what-sells-2026.md).

### Stage 1: shows and online

and lower-value cards online, and keep a log of every buy and sale. This stage
shows what collections cost, what sells, and what local buyers pay, and it
builds the TCGplayer seller level the store will use: new sellers start at 100
items and $1,000 listed and reach the top level after 51 orders
([online and legal](research/online-and-legal.md), section 1.2, fact). Card
shows are the fastest channel in 2026, and the best place to buy
([what sells in 2026](research/what-sells-2026.md), section 5).

Set-up items: a written OK from city Planning for shipping orders from home (or
another shipping address), and a look at the state and county rules for buying
used goods from the public.

**Fair dealing** (plan). Profit comes from volume, buying well and fast
turnover, not from squeezing customers:

- **Posted buy prices.** The buy rates below are posted at the counter and
  online, the same for every seller, kids included, and every offer is shown
  against the TCGplayer market price.
- **Store credit pays more than cash.** Credit offers run above the cash rates;
  Card Kingdom pays 30% more in credit than cash
  ([online and legal](research/online-and-legal.md), section 1.9).
- **Singles at market.** Singles are priced at TCGplayer market or a little
  under, never above, with dollar and quarter boxes for kids and new players.
- **Hot releases go to players first.** Allocated product is sold at a posted
  price, near list price, with a limit per customer and first access for
  league and event players, not marked up to secondary-market prices.
- **No repacks, mystery boxes or breaks.** They carry lottery claims and
  legal risk ([what sells in 2026](research/what-sells-2026.md), section 5).
- **Honest condition and authentic slabs.** Condition is graded honestly,
  misgraded cards come back, and every slab is checked against counterfeits.

None of this lowers the plan's margins: they already assume market prices for
singles and sealed product near list price.

Online margins (estimate): a $25 card bought at 65% nets 13.9% on TCGplayer and
18.5% on eBay Live after fees and postage; a $5 card bought at 40% nets 21.2%
and 25.7%. Split evenly between the two, online singles earn about 18.9%.
High-value cards bought at 80% sell in the store or at shows; shipped, they
lose money.

## 6. Stock and cash

At five turns a year and a 71.4% cost ratio, $6,417 a month of sales needs
6,417 x 12 x 0.7142 / 5 = $11,000 of stock at cost: the opening stock. Five
turns is a guess: one store owner's rule ties five turns to a 45% margin
([category and unit economics](research/category-and-unit-economics.md),
section 3), and stage 1 measures the real rate. At four turns break-even needs
$13,749 of stock; at three, $18,332. Each
$3,000 a month of profit adds $12,317 a month of sales and $21,113 of stock.
The operating cash covers three to six months of costs with no sales at all.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | What the plan does |
|------|--------------------|
| Collections cost more than planned | Tiered, posted buy rates; store credit offers; high-value cards sell in store and at shows |
| New sealed product loses its premium | Sealed bought to event demand and sold through; One Piece is the only deep sealed line |
| Card prices drop after big releases | Buy chase singles after a set's first weeks; no speculative stock |
| Counterfeit slabs and cards | Check every slab and high-value card; buy slabs from known sources |
| Theft | Two people on event nights; high-value stock in the safe overnight; collectibles covered by insurance |
| Lease commitment ($24,444 over 3 years) | Cap any personal guarantee in the lease |
| Missed LLC filings | Calendar the Wyoming annual report and the Indiana entity report |
| Online platform rules change | Four channels: TCGplayer, eBay, eBay Live, Whatnot |

The owners review sales at month 9 and decide whether to adjust, grow or wind
down.
