# Storefront Plan

> Last updated: 2026-10-02
> Status: draft. Built from the six research files and the owner's operating
> choices. No quote, count or sales figure in it has been collected yet; the
> checks in section 2 exist to collect them.

## Summary

A card store on Franklin Street: 80% trading card games, 20% sports cards,
events most nights, run full time by the owner with a partner on event nights,
both unpaid for up to six months. It is owned through a Wyoming parent LLC that
owns an Indiana LLC, which runs the store. It starts small and adds money in
stages. Stage 1, buying and selling at card shows and online with no lease,
costs $11,775 to $21,775. Stage 2, an 858 sq ft store with 20 seats, costs
$39,278 to $52,614 before credit for cards carried in from stage 1. Both stages
together come to about $46,500 at the minimum and about $65,900 at the full
level, not counting a reserve for overruns or the owner's living costs. The
store breaks even at about $6,300 a month in sales, needs about $11,600 to pay
the partner and about $23,400 to pay both: 92% of the area's estimated card
spending at the 2025 peak, and more than all of it at the 2024 level. Nine
checks (gates, section 2) must pass before any lease.

Every figure carries a confidence tier as defined on the [overview](README.md).
Figures marked "plan" are choices this plan makes; figures marked "owner" are
the owner's own observations or decisions.

### Ruled out by the research

As a lasting business, online-only is ruled out: distributors refuse
online-only sellers, and cards bought at 80% lose money on TCGplayer. Stage 1
sells online only the lower-value cards bought well below that. A counter
inside another business is also ruled out: Southern Hobby refuses stores
operating within another business, and WPN requires a store that does not
share space ([distribution and supply](research/distribution-and-supply.md),
sections 1.2 and 2.1).

## Headline, pessimistic first

1. **The store covers its costs at $6,290 a month in sales while nobody is
   paid. Paying the partner raises that to $11,558; paying both raises it to
   $23,359** (section 5, estimate built on guesses). $23,359 a month is about
   92% of the trade area's estimated hobby card spend at the 2025 peak (guess).
   A store that pays two people needs customers from well beyond Michigan City.
2. **Buy rates set the margin.** Paying 80% of market on every card and selling
   at market leaves 20% on singles. This plan pays 80% only on cards worth $50
   or more and less on cheaper cards, which blends to 63.75% of market value
   and a 36.3% singles margin (section 1, guess). If collections come in mostly
   high-value, the margin moves toward 20%.
3. **Cards bought at 80% cannot be resold online at a profit.** A $100 card
   bought for $80 nets $78.81 on TCGplayer after fees and postage
   ([online and legal](research/online-and-legal.md), section 1.8, estimate).
   High-value buys sell in the store or at shows.
4. **Opening the store takes $39,278 to $52,614** (section 4, estimate built
   on guesses), less credit for cards carried in from stage 1. The minimum
   stocks the store for break-even while both are unpaid; the full level adds
   the stock the partner's pay needs. Sales have to grow by about $5,300 a
   month before month 7 or the partner stays unpaid.
5. **A for-profit business generally cannot use unpaid employees.** The
   partner's six unpaid months work only if he is an owner of the business
   (general knowledge, not checked against a source). Gate G9 settles it in
   writing before opening, together with the LLC structure.

What the structure does and does not change (general knowledge, not checked
against a source; gate G9 confirms each point):

- The Indiana LLC collects Indiana sales tax and its income is taxed as
  Indiana business income; the Wyoming parent does not change that.
- A personal guarantee on the lease or on distributor credit reaches the owner
  personally, whatever the structure.
- If the partner becomes a member of either LLC, that LLC files a partnership
  tax return, which raises accounting costs.
- If the Wyoming LLC itself does business in Indiana, it registers there as a
  foreign LLC: $125 (Indiana Foreign Registration Statement, State Form 56369,
  revised 01-26, fact).

2025 was a growth spike; at the 2024 level (about $1.44B nationally) the local
figure is about $180,000, and the both-paid level exceeds it. Sports-card spend
is not in this figure. Whether ICv2's hobby channel figure includes online
hobby sales is not stated in the research.

## 6. Stock and cash

A store that sells through its stock five times a year (a plan assumption), at
a 69.8% cost ratio (1 - 30.2%, rounded), needs 6,290 x 12 x 0.698 / 5 = $10,537
of stock at cost for $6,290 a month in sales: the minimum level. One store
owner's rule in the research says a margin under 40% needs about ten turns a
year, so five is optimistic. Paying the partner needs $11,558 a month and
$19,361 of stock, the full level. Paying both needs $23,359 a month and $39,130
of stock, a stage 3 step.

The operating cash covers three to six months of costs with zero sales. The
build-out months pay rent, utilities, internet and alarm from the build-out
occupancy line in section 4; the store's other monthly costs (phone, software,
accounting, workers comp, supplies) start at opening, with the operating cash.
The rent-free build-out credit counts two months of rent; if the landlord
waives all three build-out months, the saving is three months.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Size | What the plan does |
|------|------|--------------------|
| Buy rates drift up | 80% on every card cuts the margin after fees from 25.4% to 18.1% | Tiered rates; log every buy by value tier (G3); test store credit at a higher rate than cash |
| Labor law | Unpaid work by a non-owner employee is generally not allowed (general knowledge) | Gate G9 before opening |
| Business structure | Two LLCs mean two sets of filings; a missed Wyoming annual report leads to dissolution after 60 days | Calendar both filings; gate G9 confirms the structure before forming it |
| Cost overrun | Build-out alone runs $5,000 to $25,000 in the research | G4 and G8; choose a bay with an accessible restroom; negotiate a rent-free build-out |
| Theft and burglary | $5,000 (distraction theft, Chesterton) to $340,000 (burglary, 90 miles away) per incident | Two people on event nights; high-value singles and slabs to the safe overnight; insurer's security specification; collectibles theft limit in the policy (G4) |
| Singles lose value | 30th Celebration Special Illustration singles fell 40% to 50%, one card over 80% | Buy chase cards of a new set only after its first four weeks; cap any single card at 5% of singles stock (plan) |
| Sealed loses value | Pokémon boxes and ETBs fell 17% to 24% within two months of release; OP-13 fell 36%; one Magic set fell below cost within days | Tier 1 sealed only, bought to event demand; no speculative stock |
| Sports supply | No direct sealed account available to a new store | Singles and graded cards from buys; apply to the Topps Hobby Prospect Program once the store has sales history |
| Competition | Reliquary's events 11.0 mi away; the monthly 100-table FOP show in town; Heroes Haven could add Pokémon or One Piece | G2 and G7 measure it before a lease |
| Counterfeit and stolen cards in public buys | No rate found; Bandai warns of counterfeits at event venues | ID and log every seller; refuse sealed product from individuals; refuse when unsure (plan) |
| Owner income and key person | Once the partner is paid, the owner keeps nothing until sales pass $11,558 a month | G6 funds 24 months of living costs |
| Lease after failure | $24,444 over a 3-year term | G5 caps any personal guarantee; wind-down rule in section 2 |
| Category downturn | 2025 was a growth spike; the 2024 base is about 40% lower | G7 uses current local spend, not the national trend |
