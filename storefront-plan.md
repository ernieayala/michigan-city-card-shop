# Storefront Plan

> Last updated: 2026-10-04
> Status: draft built from the six research files and the owners' choices.
> Quotes and local counts come next (section 2).

## Summary

A card store on Franklin Street run by two co-owners: the owner full time and
a partner on event nights, with no employees. Sales are 80% trading card games
and 20% sports cards, with events most nights, and the store sells online on
TCGplayer, eBay and Whatnot as well as over the counter. A Wyoming parent LLC
owns the Indiana LLC that runs the store. The owners do not need income from
it, so the financial target is to cover costs and grow.

It starts small. Stage 1, buying and selling at card shows and online, costs
$11,775 to $21,775. Stage 2, an 858 sq ft store with 20 seats, costs $40,470 to
$44,874. The store breaks even at about $6,800 a month in sales, about 23% of
the area's estimated card spending. Profit goes back into stock until the
owners choose to take any out.

Plan figures are estimates built on guesses unless a table marks them
otherwise; confidence tiers are defined on the [overview](README.md).

### Stage 1: shows and online

Buy TCG and sports collections at card shows, sell high-value cards at shows
and lower-value cards online, and keep a log of every buy and sale. This stage
shows what collections cost, what sells, and what local buyers pay, and it
builds the TCGplayer seller level the store will use: new sellers start at 100
items and $1,000 listed and reach the top level after 51 orders
([online and legal](research/online-and-legal.md), section 1.2, fact).

Set-up items: a written OK from city Planning for shipping orders from home (or
another shipping address), and a look at the state and county rules for buying
used goods from the public.

**Online sales channels:**

| Channel | What sells there | Fees | Source |
|---------|------------------|------|--------|
| TCGplayer | TCG singles bought at 65% of market or less | 10.75% commission and 2.5% + $0.30 per order; listing through CrystalCommerce's sync costs about one point more | [online and legal](research/online-and-legal.md), section 1.1 (fact) |

A storefront earns TCGplayer's Certified Hobby Shop badge, which puts its
listings in the Verified Seller filter, and TCGplayer Pro charges no fee on
in-store pickup orders ([online and legal](research/online-and-legal.md),
sections 3.1 and 7.4, fact).

Online, a $25 card bought at 65% nets 13.9% after TCGplayer fees and postage,
and a $5 card bought at 40% nets 21.2%: about 16.6% blended. A $100 card bought
at 80% loses 1.7% shipped, so high-value buys sell in the store or at shows.

## 6. Stock and cash

At five turns a year and a 72.7% cost ratio, $6,786 a month of sales needs
6,786 x 12 x 0.72678 / 5 = $11,837 of stock at cost: the opening stock. Each
$3,000 a month of profit adds $13,025 a month of sales and $22,719 of stock.
The operating cash covers three to six months of costs with no sales at all.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | What the plan does |
|------|--------------------|
| Collections cost more than planned | Tiered buy rates; store credit offers; high-value cards sell in store |
| New stores get little of the hottest product | Singles-first mix; events; allocation grows with purchase history |
| Card prices drop after big releases | Buy chase singles after a set's first weeks; no speculative sealed stock |
| Theft | Two people on event nights; high-value stock in the safe overnight; collectibles covered by insurance |
| Lease commitment ($24,444 over 3 years) | Cap any personal guarantee in the lease |
| Missed LLC filings | Calendar the Wyoming annual report and the Indiana entity report |
| Online platform rules change | Three channels: TCGplayer, eBay, Whatnot |

The owners review sales at month 9 and decide whether to adjust, grow or wind
down.
