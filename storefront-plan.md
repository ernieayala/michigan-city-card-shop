# Storefront Plan on $50,000

> Last updated: 2026-10-02
> Status: draft. Built from the six research files and the owner's operating
> choices. No quote, count or sales figure in it has been collected yet; the
> gates in section 2 exist to collect them.

## Summary

A card store in an 858 sq ft bay on Franklin Street: 80% trading card games,
20% sports cards, events most nights, run full time by the owner with a
partner on event nights, both unpaid for up to six months. On $50,000 it breaks
even at about $6,200 a month in sales, needs about $11,500 to pay the partner,
and about $23,300 to pay both, close to all the card spending the area is
estimated to have. Buying at 80% only on cards worth $50 or more keeps the
singles margin near 36%; paying 80% on everything cuts it to 20%. Section 9
compares five ways to use the money. The recommended path is a year of selling
at shows and online to measure real sales and buy rates, then opening with
more stock and less cash. Nine gates must pass before any lease.

Every figure carries a confidence tier as defined on the [overview](README.md).
Figures marked "plan" are choices this plan makes; figures marked "owner" are
the owner's own observations or decisions.

## Headline, pessimistic first

1. **The store covers its costs at $6,231 a month in sales while nobody is
   paid. Paying the partner raises that to $11,499; paying both raises it to
   $23,300** (section 5, estimate built on guesses). $23,300 a month is about
   92% of the trade area's estimated hobby card spend at the 2025 peak (guess).
   A store that pays two people needs customers from well beyond Michigan City.
2. **Buy rates set the margin.** Paying 80% of market on every card and selling
   at market leaves 20% on singles. This plan pays 80% only on cards worth $50
   or more and less on cheaper cards, which blends to 63.75% of market value
   and a 36.3% singles margin (section 1, guess). If collections come in mostly
   high-value, the margin moves toward 20%.
3. **Cards bought at 80% cannot be resold online at a profit.** A $100 card
   bought for $80 nets $78.81 on TCGplayer after fees and postage
   ([online and legal](research/online-and-legal.md), section 1.8, estimate).
   High-value buys sell in the store or at shows.
4. **$50,000 opens the store with $5,499 of inventory above break-even while
   unpaid, and $3,325 short of it once the partner is paid** (section 6,
   estimate built on guesses). Sales have to grow by about $5,300 a month
   before month 7 or the partner stays unpaid.
5. **A for-profit business generally cannot use unpaid employees.** The
   partner's six unpaid months work only if he is an owner of the business
   (general knowledge, not checked against a source). Gate G9 settles it in
   writing before opening.

## 6. Inventory and cash

At 5 turns a year and a 69.8% cost ratio (1 - 30.2%), sales of $6,231 a month
need 6,231 x 12 x 0.698 / 5 = $10,438 of inventory at cost (estimate; 5 turns
is a plan assumption, and the one store owner's rule in the research says a
margin under 40% needs about ten). Opening inventory of $15,937 supports
5 x 15,937 / 0.698 / 12 = $9,514 a month. Paying the partner needs $11,499 a
month and $19,262 of inventory; paying both needs about $39,000. Gross profit
funds that growth only after sales rise.

The operating cash covers six months of costs with zero sales. The build-out
months are paid from the pre-opening occupancy line in section 4, so the six
months start at opening. A rent-free build-out (gate G5) returns $1,358 to
inventory.

All figures are estimates built on guesses.

- **A** balances stock and runway. Its stock covers break-even but not the
  partner's pay until sales grow.
- **B** is the only storefront version whose stock supports sales near the
  $11,499 the partner's pay needs. It has three months of operating cash
  instead of six.
- **C** absorbs a cost overrun, but its stock barely supports break-even sales.
- **D** has a larger sales floor; more than 20 seats needs a second exit.
  Without a rent-free build-out it needs about $2,200 more.
- **E** puts the least money at risk: about $20,800, most of it in cards that
  can be resold. Year one has to clear about $4,800 of profit to fund version A
  in year two, which takes about $32,200 of sales at a 33% margin after card
  fees, about $1,340 a show across 24 shows (guess). Without a storefront there
  are no distributor accounts or sanctioned events, and the store opens in
  2028. Year-one costs: show tables $100 and travel $50 per show, two
  transient merchant licenses ($250), business property insurance $600 and
  supplies $100 a month (guess; table fees are not published), and the LLC
  ($125).

**Ruled out by the research:** online-only (distributors refuse online-only
sellers, and cards bought at 80% lose money on TCGplayer), and a counter inside
another business (Southern Hobby refuses stores operating within another
business, and WPN requires a store that does not share space;
[distribution and supply](research/distribution-and-supply.md), sections 1.2
and 2.1).

**Recommended path: E, then B.** No local sales figure exists yet, and E
measures sales and buy rates before a lease is signed. If year one clears about
$2,700 a month in sales, the store opens as B with proven stock and real buy
rates.
